# Scenario Review

For each of the 13 scenarios I accepted every recommendation and compared the generated design with the architecture the source repositories describe. An independent reviewer agent then checked the first version of this review against the engine output and the sources. It found wrong numbers in the review and real engine defects. A second check found three wrong fixes and several overstated verdicts; a third check found one wrong number, three unstated differences and four inconsistencies; a fourth check found eight more issues, led by strong-consistency designs reading from asynchronous replicas; a fifth check found more sizing and consistency gaps (the fifth-check rows); a sixth check found capacity and topology gaps (the sixth-check rows); a seventh check found flow and availability gaps (the seventh-check rows); an eighth check found cost-model gaps (the eighth-check row). All are corrected below. Every number comes from `npx tsx scripts/scenario-summary.ts` in `architect/`.

Verdicts: **matches** (same building blocks for the same reasons) or **difference** (stated, with the reason).

## Review of all 31 scenarios (round 7, 2026-10-19)

Four reviewer agents covered the 31 scenarios (about 8 each) with all recommendations accepted. One adversarial verifier per reviewer tried to refute each finding by reproduction, provider practice and materiality.

- 27 findings reported: 22 confirmed, 5 refuted (Bedrock Guardrails per-call price, log ingestion share, flat on-prem Loki and WAF, a Power BI tie on AWS, Keyspaces priced as nodes).
- All 22 fixed with a pinning test, plus 3 follow-ups the fixers found: own CDN servers kept off the cloud support bill, saved unit prices migrated after an offer rename, no migration engineering when agents read a kept system live. Tests grew from 1082 to 1103.
- Largest corrections: staging no longer copies an own CDN edge fleet (video-streaming, -$744k/mo); an active-active peer region no longer copies per-request fees (social-network, -$794k/mo); a 12 TB database migrated to managed PostgreSQL stays on one instance; OIDC is no longer recommended at 10M daily users (social-graph, $101k to $5.3k/mo); IoT devices reach the stream through a device gateway with per-device identity; WebSocket designs bill held load-balancer connections.
- Finding list and fixes: [review-round7-findings.md](review-round7-findings.md). The per-scenario rows below predate round 7 for the numbers these fixes moved; `npx tsx scripts/scenario-summary.ts` prints the current designs.

## Exhaustive review (sixth round)

A multi-agent workflow reviewed all 13 designs through six lenses: capacity, data layer, reliability and cost, graph coherence, source fidelity and engine logic. Three independent checkers judged each new finding by reproduction, practice and materiality, and a finding counted only with at least two confirmations.

- 6 rounds, 654 agents: 154 findings confirmed (9 high, 75 medium, 70 low) and 52 rejected.
- The review did not converge: round 6 still found 15 new findings. Treat the list as broad, not complete.
- Fixes: 144 fixed with a pinning test, 10 already covered by an earlier fix. Independent checkers then confirmed all 154 resolved (149 on the first check, 5 after one repair pass). The test suite grew from 168 to 311 tests.
- High-severity fixes: strong consistency no longer fails over to an asynchronous standby; object storage and CDN cost scale with stored volume and egress; feed fan-out deliveries flow through the queue, workers and cache; the chat and social-network inputs follow their sources; the exported Mermaid diagram renders; replication and partitioning are asked only after their inputs; search sizing covers scatter-gather over index shards.

The rows below include the fixes from all rounds.
